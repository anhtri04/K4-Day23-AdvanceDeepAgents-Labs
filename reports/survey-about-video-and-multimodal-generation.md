# Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Control, Unification, Evaluation and Deployment

## TL;DR

- Video generation has consolidated around one recipe: a video-aware tokenizer (3D VAE / causal encoder) compresses video into latents, and a diffusion transformer operating on spacetime patches denoises them under text conditioning — the paradigm Sora formalised and that Latte, W.A.L.T., CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo and Wan instantiate in open form [1][2][3][4][5][6].
- Control and long-form generation are the two axes where the field diverges most: plug-and-play condition modules on frozen backbones give camera/structure control, while long video is split between training-free sliding-window co-denoising and autoregressive causal streaming, the latter increasingly distilled down to 3–4 sampling steps for real-time playback [7][8][9][10][11][12].
- Unified any-to-any models — Chameleon, Emu3, Transfusion, Show-o, Janus-Pro, Uni-MoE-2.0-Omni — converge on "one transformer over all modalities" but disagree on the objective: fully autoregressive discrete tokens, autoregressive text plus continuous diffusion, hybrid mask-based discrete diffusion / discrete flow matching, or MoE routing [13][14][15][16][17][18][19].
- Evaluation is the weakest link: FVD's I3D features are non-Gaussian, temporally insensitive and sample-hungry, CLIPScore behaves like a "bag of words" on compositional prompts, and leaderboards mix self-reported numbers with configurations that are explicitly "not directly comparable" [20][21][22][23][24].
- Deployment pressure is now coming from efficiency and provenance simultaneously: stacked distillation + low-bit quantisation + sparse attention report 100–200x speedups on video diffusion, while C2PA Content Credentials, SynthID video watermarking and the EU AI Act's marking mandate push provenance into the generation pipeline itself [25][26][27][28][29].

## Background

"Video and multimodal generation" covers systems that synthesise temporally coherent video, and more recently systems that generate across several modalities — text, images, video, audio — inside one model. The field matters now because three curves crossed at once: latent diffusion transformers made video quality scale with compute [30][1], open-weight video models such as HunyuanVideo (13B, the largest open-source video model at its release) and Wan (14B/1.3B) put frontier capability in public hands [5][6], and open training pipelines such as Open-Sora 2.0 demonstrated a commercial-level video model trained for only $200k [31]. Meanwhile unified multimodal models moved from "understand images" to "generate them", with GPT-4o's native image generation in March 2025 explicitly framing one autoregressive transformer that models the joint distribution of text and pixels [32], and Movie Gen generating 1080p video with synchronised audio from a 30B-parameter transformer with 73K-token context [33].

The technical story starts from image latent diffusion. DiT showed that replacing the U-Net with a transformer over latent patches scales predictably — deeper/wider models and more tokens gave monotonically lower FID, with DiT-XL/2 reaching FID 2.27 on ImageNet 256x256 [30]. Video models then extended this in two directions. The first added a temporal dimension to an existing image LDM: VideoLDM/Align-your-Latents pretrained an image LDM and fine-tuned it on encoded image sequences to reach up to 1280x2048 video [34], and Stable Video Diffusion formalised a three-stage curriculum of text-to-image pretraining, video pretraining and high-quality video finetuning with heavy captioning/filtering of the pretraining set [35]. The second built purpose-made transformer backbones over spatiotemporal tokens: Latte extracted spatio-temporal tokens and studied factorised spatial/temporal attention variants [2]; W.A.L.T used a causal encoder to jointly compress images and videos into one latent space plus window attention and a three-model cascade (base + two video super-resolution stages) producing 512x896 at 8 fps [3]; CogVideoX paired a 3D VAE that compresses along both spatial and temporal axes with an "expert transformer" using expert adaptive LayerNorm for text-video fusion and progressive training [4].

Sora defined the reference formulation: videos are compressed into a lower-dimensional latent, decomposed into spacetime patches that serve as transformer tokens, and a text-conditional diffusion transformer is trained jointly on videos and images of variable duration, resolution and aspect ratio at native size, with DALL·E-3-style re-captioning and GPT-based prompt expansion [1]. The costs this creates explain most downstream design choices: the extra temporal axis multiplies token counts, so systems trade compression ratio, attention factorisation (joint vs factorised vs windowed vs sliding-tile), patching strategy and model scale against quality and inference latency.

## Theme 1: Video tokenizers and diffusion-transformer backbones

Across the open literature the same two-part anatomy recurs. A video-aware tokenizer (3D VAE or causal 3D encoder) maps a clip into a compact latent, and a diffusion transformer denoises patch/tubelet tokens conditioned on text embeddings. Wan 2.1's high-level design is described exactly this way — a Wan-VAE 3D causal VAE encoding video/image into a latent, N diffusion-transformer blocks attending to umT5 text embeddings via cross-attention, then a decoder [36]; CogVideoX's 3D VAE compresses both spatially and temporally with an expert transformer fusing text and video [4]; W.A.L.T's causal encoder jointly covers images and video in one latent space [3].

Backbone design is where the variants diverge. Latte explicitly studies decomposing the spatial and temporal dimensions into efficient attention variants and probes clip patch embedding, temporal positional embedding and timestep-class injection [2]. W.A.L.T replaces full spatiotemporal attention with window attention tailored for joint spatial and spatiotemporal modelling, then relies on a cascade of super-resolution diffusion stages to reach high resolution [3]. HunyuanVideo 1.5 at 8.3B parameters uses selective and sliding tile attention (SSTA) with glyph-aware text encoding for bilingual prompts and a video super-resolution network, unified across text-to-video and image-to-video [37]. Wan emphasises a novel VAE plus scalable pretraining and large-scale data curation, with the 1.3B variant running in only 8.19 GB VRAM and the 14B variant used to demonstrate video-generation scaling laws with respect to data and model size [6]. HunyuanVideo's 13B system packages data curation, architecture, progressive scaling and infrastructure into one framework [5]. Stable Video Diffusion notably reports that its strong motion representation also transfers to image-to-video and serves as a multi-view 3D prior, hinting at the world-model framing [35] that Sora makes explicit — Sora argues diffusion transformers scale effectively as video models and that training at native aspect ratios improves framing and composition [1]. Open-Sora 2.0 keeps this architecture but attacks cost, reporting 5–10x better cost-efficiency than comparable systems such as MovieGen and Step-Video-T2V while remaining comparable to HunyuanVideo and Runway Gen-3 Alpha on human evaluation and VBench [31].

## Theme 2: Controllability — camera, motion and structure on frozen backbones

Controllability work is remarkably consistent in shape: add a small, plug-and-play conditioning module to a frozen video diffusion model rather than retraining the base. CameraCtrl trains a camera-pose module on top of a video diffusion model leaving other modules untouched, parameterising trajectories with Plücker embeddings injected into temporal attention [7]; MotionCtrl goes further by decoupling camera motion from object motion in one unified controller [38]. Structural conditioning reuses the ControlNet recipe in the temporal domain: ControlVideo is a training-free adaptation that improves appearance coherence and reduces flicker for long-video production [39], and CCEdit decouples video structure from appearance, using ControlNet for structure preservation with adaptable temporal modules and personalisation for controllable editing [40]. Gen-L-Video provides the multi-text interface for long generation by temporal co-denoising rather than autoregression [8][41].

Identity and subject personalisation follow two distinct strategies. The tuning route fine-tunes an image diffusion model per subject or per video — Tune-A-Video does one-shot tuning on a single video-text pair and is inherently compatible with DreamBooth, T2I-Adapter and ControlNet, so control comes at no extra training cost [42]. The inference-time route avoids optimisation: Video Alchemist personalises multiple open-set subjects inside a diffusion transformer via cross-attention without test-time optimisation, outperforming prior methods [43]. Story-level consistency is typically handled by attention or memory mechanisms instead of tuning: StoryDiffusion uses Consistent Self-Attention to keep characters consistent over long-range sequences and a Semantic Motion Predictor to turn condition images into long video [44], while StoryMem adds a memory bank on top of pretrained single-shot video diffusion models for cinematic multi-shot output [45].

## Theme 3: Long-form, streaming and interactive video

Long video splits into two philosophically different families. The training-free family treats a long clip as one long token sequence: Gen-L-Video's temporal co-denoising extends off-the-shelf models that typically handle under 24 frames to hundreds of frames with diverse semantic segments, explicitly to avoid autoregressive error accumulation [8][41]. The autoregressive family instead generates causally with memory. StreamingT2V is autoregressive and reaches 80, 240, 600, 1200 or more frames with smooth transitions using a short-term Conditional Attention Module and a long-term Appearance Preservation Module plus randomised blending in an autoregressive video enhancer [9]. MAGI-1 generates video by autoregressively predicting fixed-length chunks, trained to denoise per-chunk noise that increases monotonically over time; its largest variant has 24B parameters and supports context lengths up to 4 million tokens while maintaining constant peak inference cost regardless of video length [11]. A secondary write-up describes the chunks as 24 frames (≈1s at 24fps) and reports first-chunk latency of about 2.3s with subsequent chunks under 1s, and notes chunk-wise prompting for smooth scene transitions and long-horizon synthesis [46].

The bottleneck for autoregressive video is the train-test gap: models trained on short clips must stream indefinitely without error accumulation. A family of distillation methods attacks this directly. A causal-distillation line converts pretrained bidirectional diffusion transformers into autoregressive ones: the work behind CausVid distils a 50-step model into a 4-step generator by extending distribution-matching distillation, scoring 84.27 on VBench-Long and streaming at 9.4 FPS on a single GPU with KV caching; its student is initialised from the teacher's ODE trajectories, and an asymmetric strategy supervises the causal student with a bidirectional teacher [10]. Self Forcing reduces exposure bias via holistic video-level supervision and efficient caching [47]; Rolling Forcing adds joint denoising and an attention-sink mechanism with non-overlapping-window training [48]; Causal Forcing uses AR teachers for ODE initialisation in distillation [49]. Applied to interactivity, LongLive combines causal attention, KV-recache, streaming long tuning and short-window attention for frame-level real-time long generation [12].

## Theme 4: Unifying modalities in one model

The unification question is: how do you put text, images, video and audio into one generator? Surveys of the area (Zhang et al. and the Omni-MLLM survey) group the answers into diffusion-based, autoregressive-based and hybrid designs, and describe the tension plainly — text-to-image has been dominated by diffusion (UNet/DiT with CLIP/T5 encoders) while multimodal understanding is dominated by autoregressive decoder-only next-token prediction [50][51]. The 2025 surveys also flag that GPT-4o's March 2025 release crystallised the unification trend [50], and OpenAI's own framing of 4o image generation is one natively multimodal autoregressive transformer over the joint distribution of images and text, illustrated as "tokens → transformer → diffusion → pixels" with direct modelling of (text, pixels, sound) [32].

The fully autoregressive family treats new modalities as new languages. AnyGPT unifies speech, text, images and music entirely through discrete representations, training without changing the LLM architecture or paradigm — only data-level preprocessing — on a synthesised 108k-sample any-to-any instruction dataset [52]. Emu3 tokenises images, text and videos into a discrete space and trains a single transformer from scratch on next-token prediction, surpassing SDXL and LLaVA-1.6 while eliminating diffusion or compositional architectures, and generates video by predicting the next token in a video sequence [14]. Chameleon is an early-fusion token-based mixed-modal family that understands and generates images and text in arbitrary sequences with a stable from-inception training recipe, matching or exceeding Gemini Pro and GPT-4V on a long-form mixed-modal evaluation [13]. Janus-Pro improves Janus via optimised training, expanded data and larger scale, advancing both understanding and text-to-image instruction following while improving generation stability [53]. AR-Omni pushes this to a single Transformer decoder with one token stream and one next-token objective generating text, images and streaming speech — no expert decoders — using task-aware loss reweighting, a token-level perceptual alignment loss for image tokens and finite-state decoding at 0.88 real-time factor for speech [54].

The hybrid and diffusion-based family keeps text autoregressive but treats other modalities as continuous latents. Transfusion combines next-token-prediction loss for discrete text with diffusion loss for continuous image vectors in one transformer over mixed-modality sequences, scaled to 7B parameters and 2T multimodal tokens [15]. Show-o is a single transformer combining autoregressive and discrete-diffusion modelling to handle mixed-modality inputs and outputs — VQA, text-to-image, inpainting/extrapolation and mixed-modality generation — in one model [16]. A more radical variant replaces the backbone itself: Omni-Diffusion is presented as the first any-to-any multimodal language model built entirely on mask-based discrete diffusion, capturing a joint distribution over discrete multimodal tokens across text, speech and images [18], while NExT-OMNI achieves unified any-to-any understanding and generation through discrete flow matching (metric-induced probability paths, kinetic optimal velocities) trained on large-scale interleaved text, image, video and audio data [19]. A third family scales capacity per modality: Uni-MoE uses sparse mixture-of-experts with modality-specific encoders and connectors plus modality-level data parallelism and expert-level model parallelism in a three-stage progressive curriculum [55], and Uni-MoE-2.0-Omni turns a dense LLM into an omnimodal model with dynamic-capacity MoE and a progressive alignment → expert warm-up → MoE fine-tuning/RL → generative-training strategy [17]. Omnimodal surveys compress all of this into a taxonomy of continuous vs discrete vs hybrid encoding and text-token vs modality-token vs representation-based generation organisation, with modality expansion, catastrophic forgetting and temporal alignment as the standing challenges [51][50].

Audio-video generation is the clearest stress test of unification. Movie Gen generates 1080p video with synchronised audio and reports state-of-the-art on text-to-video, video personalisation, video editing, video-to-audio and text-to-audio [33]. Harmony targets synchronised audio-video generation with cross-task synergy training, naming Correspondence Drift, inefficient global attention and intra-modal classifier-free-guidance bias as the failure modes of naive joint diffusion [56]. Yet unification is not free: a 2026 study finds that state-of-the-art unified models such as UniLIP, Emu3 and Janus can produce correct textual answers while exhibiting "catastrophic collapse" when asked for the corresponding visual answer [57].

## Theme 5: Evaluation — metrics, benchmarks and their failure modes

Evaluation stacks three generations of methods. The old distributional layer — FVD computed from I3D embeddings, alongside FID and IS on testbeds such as UCF-101 and MSR-VTT — remains in use [58][59], but its foundations are contested. One study identifies three limitations of FVD: the I3D feature space is non-Gaussian, I3D features are insensitive to temporal distortions, and reliable estimation needs impractically large samples; it proposes JEDi, which needs only 16% of the samples and raises human-evaluation alignment by 34% on average [23]. The original FVD work, conversely, argued FVD was the best of the metrics tested and well-matched to human judgment in a ranking study — evidence that this dispute is not settled [58]. A large human experiment on image generative models found no current metric, FID included, strongly correlates with human assessment of realism [60], and practical pitfalls are catalogued as low-N FID unreliability, non-comparable FID across resolutions, single-seed reporting and CLIP-score inflation [24].

The alignment layer (CLIPScore) has its own defect: CLIPScore "fails to produce reliable scores for complex prompts involving compositions of objects, attributes, and relations" because CLIP text encoders can act as a bag of words; VQAScore instead computes the probability of a "Yes" answer to "Does this figure show '{text}'?" and is open-sourced with GenAI-Bench [22]. On GenAI-Bench's 1,600 compositional prompts requiring scene, object, attribute, relationship and higher-order reasoning parsing, VQAScore significantly outperforms previous metrics, and ranking by VQAScore is 2x–3x more effective than PickScore, HPSv2 and ImageReward at improving human alignment [21]. cFreD argues neither extreme suffices — FID captures image quality but ignores text conditioning while CLIPScore is insensitive to visual quality — and unifies both in a Conditional Fréchet Distance [61]. T2VScore was likewise proposed because FVD, IS and CLIP score give an incomplete analysis, especially of temporal content, integrating Text-Video Alignment and Video Quality and validated on 2,543 human-judged generated videos [62].

The benchmark layer is where the field now does most of its comparing. VBench disentangles video generation quality into 16 dimensions — subject consistency, background consistency, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, object class, human action, colour, spatial relationship, scene, appearance style, temporal style and others — each with tailored prompts and evaluation methods, plus released human-preference annotations to validate alignment with human perception [63]. VBench-2.0 shifts from superficial faithfulness to "intrinsic faithfulness" across Human Fidelity, Controllability, Creativity, Physics and Commonsense, each broken into fine-grained capabilities, integrating generalist VLMs/LLMs with specialists such as anomaly detectors and extensive human-judgment annotations; its leaderboard computes Total, Creativity, Commonsense, Controllability, Human Fidelity and Physics scores [64][65]. EvalCrafter criticises judging conditional generators by FVD or IS alone and builds a 700-prompt list from real user data evaluated on visual, content and motion qualities plus text-caption alignment with about 18 objective metrics, fitting coefficients to align objective metrics with user opinion and reporting higher correlation than simple averaging [66]. FETV adds fine-grained temporal-aware prompt categorisation along major content, controlled attributes and prompt complexity, and finds CLIPScore and FVD correlate poorly with human evaluation [67]. T2V-CompBench is the first benchmark tailored to compositional text-to-video, covering consistent and dynamic attribute binding, spatial relationships, motion binding, action binding, object interactions and generative numeracy across 1,400 prompts with MLLM-, detection- and tracking-based metrics validated against human evaluation [68]. Physics-IQ probes physical understanding by comparing generated video against real-world physics experiments across Solid Mechanics, Fluid Dynamics, Optics, Thermodynamics and Magnetism; a follow-up audit refined 57.6% of samples and improved over 34.8% of prompts while observing only moderate ranking changes (Kendall's τ = 0.46) across six image-to-video models [69]. Audio-video generation required its own suite: VABench covers three task types (text-to-audio-video, image-to-audio-video, stereo audio-video) and 15 dimensions including text-video/text-audio/video-audio pairwise similarity, audio-video synchronisation and lip-speech consistency [70].

Human evaluation is widely considered superior to automatic metrics but was historically unreproducible: T2VHE adds well-defined metrics, annotator training and a dynamic evaluation module, reporting nearly 50% lower evaluation cost while improving reproducibility [71]. Governance of reported numbers is still weak: the VBench FAQ documents three submission routes (team-handled evaluation, self-submitted `eval_results.zip`, and open/closed submissions with API access for closed models) and states each leaderboard entry records who performed sampling and evaluation [72], while third-party leaderboards label entries "Self-reported" [73] and model documentation warns that "results from different evaluation configurations are not directly comparable" when citing VBench scores [20]. Prompt-set bias, prompt contamination, Elo bias from prompt overlap and human-eval crowd skew round out the list of structural threats to comparability [24][60].

## Theme 6: Efficiency, data and provenance

Efficiency gains now come from stacking rather than any single trick. TurboDiffusion reports 100–200x end-to-end speedup with comparable quality by combining low-bit SageAttention, trainable Sparse-Linear Attention, rCM step distillation and W8A8 quantisation on a single RTX 5090, cutting typical sampling from ~100 steps to 3–4 and latency on Wan2.1-T2V-14B-720P from 4767s to 24s [25][74]. Quantisation itself was specialised for diffusion transformers: ViDiT-Q achieves W8A8 with negligible degradation and a metric-decoupled W4A8 mixed-precision mode with 2–2.5x memory saving and 1.4–1.7x end-to-end latency speedup [26]. Attention sparsification is the third axis: Efficient-vDiT prunes 3D full attention and shortens sampling for significant speedups with minimal performance loss [75]. Model-cooperation approaches exploit the scale gap — SRDiffusion pairs Wan 14B with Wan 1.3B to keep VBench at 84.12 vs 84.05 with 3.21x speedup, and CogVideoX 5B with 2B lands within 0.04 VBench points at 1.82x speedup [76]. Step/trajectory distillation for real-time autoregressive generation (Rolling Forcing, Causal Forcing) belongs to the same effort [48][49].

Data curation is the other half of the cost equation, and it scales to tens of millions of pairs. Panda-70M curates 3.8M high-resolution videos from HD-VILA-100M, splits them into semantically consistent clips, applies multiple cross-modality teacher models for captions and then finetunes a retrieval model to select the best caption per clip, yielding 70M video-text pairs [77]. VidGen-1M uses coarse-to-fine curation with CLIP-score filtering to drop low text-video similarity pairs and LLAMA3.1 to remove pairs with low alignment or temporal consistency (scene transition, frame-level generation, reduplication) [78]; the same work notes that existing datasets such as HD-VILA-100M and Panda-70M lack precise captions, with average caption length under 15 words [78]. ViMix-14M targets the access problem with ~14M crawl-free, download-ready pairs built by merging open sources with unified de-duplication, quality filtering and multi-granularity ground-truth-guided re-captioning, in contrast to datasets that require manual YouTube crawling and suffer link rot and licensing uncertainty [79].

Provenance is becoming part of the generation stack rather than an afterthought. C2PA Content Credentials v2.4 supports live video streaming and unstructured text manifests, and "durable" Content Credentials combine a hard binding (cryptographic hashing) with a soft binding (watermarking and fingerprinting) so credentials can be recovered after metadata is stripped; the specification is careful that provenance means well-formed, tamper-free, valid and trusted data, not a judgement of truth [27]. Google DeepMind extended SynthID to watermark AI-generated video in Veo and text in Gemini, embedding a watermark directly into the pixels of every video frame without changing visible quality [28]. Regulatory pressure is explicit: the EU AI Act Article 50 requires providers to mark synthetic output in a machine-readable format detectable as artificially generated or manipulated from 2 August 2026 [29]. In-generation watermarking research follows suit — SPDMark embeds robust, imperceptible watermarks via selective parameter displacement with low-rank adaptation in video diffusion models [80]. Detection, however, lags: AEGIS is a benchmark of over 10,000 curated real and synthetic videos from Stable Video Diffusion, CogVideoX-5B, KLing and Sora, on which advanced vision-language models show limited detection capability on the hardest subsets [81], and DeeptraceReward annotates human-perceived deepfake traces in AI-generated video to train multimodal LLM detectors [82].

## Trends and open problems

**Convergence on a single video recipe, divergence in control and length.** Tokenizer + diffusion transformer + progressive training is now near-universal across closed and open systems [1][4][5][6][37][31]. The open questions have moved to how control is injected (adapters on frozen backbones vs trained-in conditioning) [7][38][39][43], and how length is handled (training-free co-denoising vs causal autoregression with memory and distillation) [8][9][11][10].

**Few-step, streaming generation is becoming the default target.** Distilling 50 steps to 4 for 9.4 FPS streaming [10], and 100 steps to 3–4 with attention and quantisation co-design [25][74], plus real-time interactive frameworks with KV-recache and streaming tuning [12], suggest that latency — not peak quality — is the binding constraint for the next generation of products.

**Unification is unresolved between token and latent, and between AR and diffusion.** Fully autoregressive discrete-token models [13][52][14][53][54], AR-text + diffusion-latent hybrids [15][16], mask-based discrete diffusion and discrete flow matching [18][19], and MoE routing [55][17] all have competent exponents; the survey literature frames this as an open taxonomy rather than a settled hierarchy [50][51]. The "catastrophic collapse" result — correct text, wrong image from the same unified model — is the sharpest empirical warning that understanding and generation are not yet the same capability [57], and joint audio-video generation adds synchronisation failure modes such as Correspondence Drift and CFG bias [56][33].

**Evaluation validity and leaderboard hygiene are the biggest credibility risks.** Metric-level problems (FVD's non-Gaussian, temporally insensitive features and sample requirements [23]; CLIPScore's bag-of-words behaviour [22]; quality-vs-alignment trade-offs [61]) combine with benchmark-level problems (self-reported entries [73], non-comparable evaluation configurations [20], and only moderate ranking stability under audit [69]). Compositional [68][21], physics [69] and audio-video [70] benchmarks are the field's response, and standardised human protocols such as T2VHE [71] are the complementary fix — but the gap between what is measured and what users perceive remains wide [60][24].

**Data licensing, caption detail and provenance are now first-class engineering constraints.** Crawl-free, well-captioned corpora [79], retrieval-selected teacher captions [77] and coarse-to-fine filtering with LLM scrutiny [78] respond to the observation that average captions under 15 words limit text-following [78]. Simultaneously, C2PA durable credentials, SynthID video watermarks and the EU AI Act's August 2026 marking obligation mean producers must emit machine-readable provenance by default [27][28][29], while detection benchmarks show that verifiers are not yet able to keep up [81][82].
