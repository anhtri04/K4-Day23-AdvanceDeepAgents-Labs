# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Survey

## TL;DR

- The field has converged on a three-level taxonomy of inference efficiency — data-level, model-level and system-level — motivated by three root causes: model size (LLaMA-2-70B needs ~140 GB of FP16 storage), the quadratic cost of attention, and autoregressive token-by-token decoding that repeatedly reloads weights and grows the KV cache [1][2][3].
- Compression is the dominant lever, but it is a trade-off rather than a free lunch: 4-bit weight-only quantization (W4A16) on LLaMA-2 yields up to 2.5x decoding speedup yet up to 1.3x *pre*filling slowdown from dequantization overhead, and current compression gains are frequently accompanied by significant quality drops [1][2].
- Serving-system work delivers the largest gains at the memory-management layer: PagedAttention-style KV paging with continuous batching improves throughput 2–4x at equal latency versus FasterTransformer/Orca, and newer quantized KV caches push context to 10M tokens or 1 bit per channel [4][5][6].
- Small language models (roughly 100M–5B parameters) are now competitive with much larger models on many benchmarks: the Phi-3 technical report describes phi-3-mini (3.8B, 3.3T tokens) reaching 69% MMLU / 8.38 MT-Bench, with the family reported as deployable quantized (4-bit) on an iPhone A16 at over 12 tokens/s, while Gemma 2's small members are distilled from a 27B teacher [7][8][9][10].
- The central open tension is that compression and evaluation interact badly at small scale: SLMQuant finds LLM-tuned outlier methods degrade *worse* on SLMs, and low-bit PTQ can cut mathematical-reasoning accuracy by up to 69.81% with degradation amplifying roughly fourfold across difficulty tiers [11][12][13].

## Background

The topic sits at the intersection of two pressures. First, large-model inference is expensive for structural reasons: a survey of efficient inference attributes inefficiency to large model size, quadratic-complexity attention, and auto-regressive decoding that reloads the entire weight set for every token while the KV cache grows [1]. Concretely, loading a LLaMA-70B model requires 140 GB of VRAM before any inference memory is allocated [2], and the roofline-style survey framing identifies hardware bottlenecks (memory bandwidth versus compute) as the organizing principle for choosing an optimization [3].

Second, a distinct lineage of work asks whether capability *requires* scale at all. The Phi line demonstrated that a 1.3B code model trained on ~6B tokens of "textbook quality" web data plus ~1B tokens of GPT-3.5-generated synthetic textbooks attains 50.6% pass@1 on HumanEval and 55.5% on MBPP, far outperforming its data budget [14][15], and Phi-3-mini later showed a 3.8B model matching Mixtral 8x7B and GPT-3.5 on MMLU and MT-Bench [9][7]. Meanwhile two 2024 surveys formalize the subfield of "small language models": one scopes transformer-based decoder-only models at 100M–5B parameters and surveys 70 open SLMs across architectures, training datasets and training algorithms while also benchmarking latency and memory [16]; another targets 59 SLMs for commonsense reasoning, in-context learning, mathematics and coding with the same on-device measurement angle [16][17]. A third, broader survey adds a taxonomy of optimization methods, benchmark datasets, evaluation metrics and open challenges [17][18]. The stakes are practical: efficient inference energy is a first-order datacenter concern, and survey work on routing and cascading treats model selection itself as an efficiency mechanism [19][20].

## Model compression: quantization, pruning, distillation

Compression techniques fall into a small number of families that the surveys consistently enumerate: architecture/structured pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation [2][21]. A "Faster and Lighter LLMs" survey describes QAT (quantization during training) versus PTQ (post-hoc clipping-range search), notes that distillation into a compact student also improves generalization and fights overfitting, and reports LLM-QAT — a data-free QAT method that queries a pretrained model to generate training data, then trains a quantized student with a distillation loss — as capable of quantizing 7B, 13B and 30M LLaMA models down to 4 bits with KV-cache quantization on top of weights and activations [2]. Mobile-oriented quantization variants target the same goal from the hardware side: MobileQuant aims for integer-only quantization of on-device LMs to cut latency and energy while preserving accuracy [22], EdgeQAT uses entropy- and distribution-guided quantization-aware training for lightweight edge LLMs [23], and Agile-Quant applies activation-guided quantization and pruning for faster edge inference [24].

The quantization trade-off is the best-quantified result in the literature. The efficient-inference survey's own comparative experiments report that for weight-only 4-bit quantization (W4A16) on LLaMA-2, decoding speeds up by up to 2.5x because fewer bytes of weights must be moved per token, but prefilling can slow by up to 1.3x because dequantization adds overhead — a decode-versus-prefill asymmetry that means quantization is not uniformly beneficial [1]. Weight-only quantization keeps computation in FP16; weight-activation quantization computes fully in lower precision [1]. At the extreme end, a genuine 1-bit PTQ framework argues that many "1-bit" methods exceed the nominal 1-bit storage target due to hidden overhead, and proposes null-space-aware binary factorization to make binarization real [25]. On the weight side, a KV-cache finding worth noting for small models is that capacity is a confound — SLMQuant, described as the first systematic benchmark for LLM compression applied to SLMs, reports that outlier-handling methods designed for large models (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) show *more* pronounced degradation at identical low-bit settings on SLMs, because of limited parameter capacity, distinct architectural traits and different training dynamics [11].

Pruning research has moved from magnitude heuristics to structured and learnable sparsity. Compresso integrates LLMs with pruning algorithms and surpasses one-shot pruning on multiple benchmarks [26]; NIRVANA balances zero-shot accuracy against fine-tuning capability using a saliency criterion and adaptive sparsity allocation [27]; MaskLLM learns semi-structured N:M sparsity with Gumbel-Softmax sampling and shows transferability across tasks [28]; and GroupMask questions whether a fixed N:M ratio in every layer is necessary, advocating layer-adaptive group-wise sparsity [29]. The survey literature also lists sparsification methods such as SparseGPT and Wanda alongside architecture pruning driven by weight magnitude, activation patterns or sensitivity [1][2]. Distillation is being revisited too: KDFP targets a research gap in *general* (knowledge-level) distillation as opposed to post-training ability distillation such as instruction following and chain-of-thought, aimed at efficient, private edge deployment [30]. In practice the two model families that lean hardest on distillation are Gemma 2, whose 2B and 9B members are distilled from the 27B model (distilling from a larger model with an equal token budget gave significant gains) [10], and Llama 3.2's 1B/3B models, which use pruning plus knowledge distillation to stay competitive while shrinking [31].

## Serving systems and KV-cache management

At the system level, the organizing insight is that KV-cache memory is the scarce resource. PagedAttention treats the KV cache like OS virtual-memory paging to cut fragmentation and redundant duplication, gives near-zero cache waste and flexible sharing across requests, and with continuous batching improves popular LLM throughput 2–4x at the same latency versus FasterTransformer and Orca — with larger gains for longer sequences, larger models and more complex decoding algorithms [4]. The 2025 survey "Taming the Titans" covers the serving design space of low latency, high throughput, memory overhead and attention compute [19], while the "LLM Inference Unveiled" survey uses a roofline model to locate bottlenecks and analyze compression, algorithmic and system-level enhancements in one frame [3]. Speculative decoding is surveyed as a two-stage draft-and-verify framework [32]: self-speculative "Draft & Verify" skips layers during drafting and verifies in a single forward pass for up to 1.73x speedup with no extra training or memory [33], and Medusa adds parallel decoding heads with tree attention to predict multiple tokens per step [34]. Dynamic model routing and cascading forms a fourth efficiency axis — choosing among independently trained models by query characteristics to balance competing deployment objectives [20].

KV-cache compression has become a subfield of its own. ZipCache identifies and quantizes salient tokens for high compression with fast generation [35]; DynamicKV adapts per-layer token retention for long-context LLMs [36]; KVQuant targets 10-million-token context with minimal loss [5]; coupled quantization compresses keys and values to 1 bit per channel by exploiting inter-channel dependence [6]; and KVTuner pursues sensitivity-aware layer-wise mixed-precision KV quantization for nearly lossless inference [37]. At the memory-hierarchy frontier, Tutti addresses long-context serving where the KV footprint exceeds GPU HBM and CPU DRAM, offloading to NVMe SSDs and fixing the fragmented-layout problem that causes many tiny random I/Os [38]. Notably, these techniques often generalize better to small models than aggressive *weight* quantization does, since the KV cache scales with sequence length rather than parameter count.

## Small language model families and training recipes

The SLM landscape divides by recipe rather than by size. The data-centric branch is the Phi family: phi-1 (1.3B, ~6B filtered web code tokens + ~1B synthetic textbook tokens, 4 days on 8 A100s) [14][15]; phi-1.5 (1.3B, 24 layers, 32 heads, head dim 64, MLP inner dim 8192, rotary dim 32, 2048 context, codegen-mono tokenizer), whose data mixed the ~7B-token phi-1 corpus with a 95B-token filtered-web variant, reaching 40.2% GSM8K versus Llama-7B's 11.0% and 34.1% HumanEval versus Llama-65B's 23.7% [39][40]; and phi-3-mini (3.8B, 3.3T tokens, vocabulary 32,064, hidden dim 3072, 32 layers), plus phi-3-small (7B, vocab 100,352) and phi-3-medium (14B, 40 heads/layers, embedding 5120, 4.8T tokens) scoring 75% and 78% MMLU [7][9]. Phi-3's data is filtered web text selected by "educational level" plus synthetic LLM-generated data, continuing the "Textbooks Are All You Need" methodology [9][41], and the family is framed as evidence that data quality can break existing scaling laws — phi-2 (2.7B) reportedly outscores LLaMA-2 13B and Mistral 7B on commonsense, math and coding, and Phi-3-Mini is argued to match LLaMA-2 70B MMLU with under 6% of the parameters [41].

The second branch is architectural and distillation-driven. Gemma 2 spans 2B/9B/27B with interleaved local-global attention and grouped-query attention, building on Gemma-1 features such as 8192-token context, RoPE and an approximated GeGLU nonlinearity, with a three-stage "Warp" model-merging technique, and reports that swapping MHA for GQA gave comparable quality with better parameter efficiency and faster inference [42][10][43]. The original Gemma family is described as lightweight open models derived from Gemini research that outperform similarly sized open models on text tasks [44]. Mistral 7B (7.3B, 32 layers, hidden 4096, 32 query heads and 8 KV heads of dim 128, FFN 14336, vocab 32000, sliding window 4096, context 8192) uses sliding-window attention that stacks across layers to attend to roughly 131K tokens with ~2x speedup over standard attention, plus a rolling buffer cache that cuts cache memory 8x on long sequences [45][46]. Llama 3.2's 1B and 3B models (3B: ~3.213B parameters, 128K context, eight languages, 9T training tokens, released 2024-09-25) are explicitly optimized for edge and mobile [47][31].

The third branch simply scales data and tokens at small size. Qwen2 spans 0.5B–72B including MoE variants, with Qwen2-72B base at 84.2 MMLU, 37.9 GPQA, 64.6 HumanEval, 89.5 GSM8K and 82.4 BBH [48]; Qwen2.5 raised pretraining from 7T to 18T tokens, uses BBPE with a 151,643-token vocabulary, expanded control tokens from 3 to 22 (including two tool tokens), and a decoder with GQA, SwiGLU, RoPE and RMSNorm pre-normalization, with 128K context support and 29+ languages [49][50]; Qwen3 integrates thinking and non-thinking modes to reduce compute while reaching SOTA [51]. SmolLM2 (1.7B) is overtrained on ~11T tokens in a multi-stage mix of web, math, code and instruction data with new FineMath, Stack-Edu and SmolTalk datasets, reporting it beats Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama3.2-1B [52]. TinyLlama (1.1B, ~1T tokens, ~3 epochs, Llama-2 architecture/tokenizer plus FlashAttention and Lit-GPT) outperforms comparable open models [53]. Post-training methods are being re-examined for this scale band too: GRPO training dynamics have been systematically studied for SLMs from 1.5B to 7B on a single 8xA100 node across math, coding and other reasoning domains [54].

## On-device and edge deployment

Deployment constraints, not benchmark scores, drive on-device design. Phones offer 50–90 GB/s of memory bandwidth against 2–3 TB/s for datacenter GPUs — a 30–50x gap — and because decoding is memory-bound (all weights are loaded per token) compression translates almost directly into throughput; high-end phones also cap usable RAM under ~4 GB, so memory rather than TOPS is the binding constraint even though mobile NPUs already reach tens of TOPS [55]. On-device inference also avoids 200–500 ms of cloud round-trip before the first token and can generate tokens in under 20 ms each at short context [55]. The practitioner recipe has converged on training in 16-bit and deploying in 4-bit, following GPTQ (2022) and AWQ (2023), with AWQ alone exceeding 19 million HuggingFace downloads; moving 16-bit to 4-bit is not merely 4x less storage but 4x less memory traffic per token [55]. Power budgets further push toward smaller, quantized, sparse models and bursty generation so the device can sleep, with always-on use cases needing single-digit milliwatts [55].

The runtime ecosystem is now mature. llama.cpp/GGUF supports ahead-of-time quantization, on-the-fly KV-cache quantization, speculative decoding and partial layer offloading across x86 (AVX/AVX2/AVX-512/AMX), ARM (Neon, i8MM, SVE/SVE2, SME), Metal, CUDA and Vulkan backends [56], with documented GGUF levels of Q4_0, Q4_K_M (the recommended quality/size balance), Q5_K_M, Q6_K, Q8_0 and F16 [57]. Measured results include ~35–50 tokens/s for a 7B model on an M2 Pro with 16 GB unified memory when offloading layers versus 12–15 tokens/s on CPU only [58], and an independent Apple Silicon comparison putting MLX-lm at 18–20 t/s and Ollama GGUF at 13 t/s, with a Gemma4-26B MoE improving from 63.9 t/s at Q4_K_M to 71.0 t/s at UD-Q2_K_XL while saving 6 GB [59]. That same harness reports TTFT diverging 100x across backends at 32k context, prefix caching giving a 626x TTFT reduction, framework-level optimization (2.4x) exceeding quantization gains (<3%), and speculative decoding reaching 206 t/s (3.2x over standalone) [60]. Q4_K_M is reported to give 80–90% of full-model quality at one-third the memory footprint, with 7B models fitting in 4 GB of RAM [61], and a 125M deep-thin model running at 50 tokens/s on an iPhone illustrates that architecture can matter more than parameter count at small scale [55].

Beyond llama.cpp, Apple MLX (open-sourced November 2023) is built around unified memory where CPU and GPU share one physical pool, eliminating device-to-device transfers, with `mlx-lm` for LLM inference, fine-tuning and quantization [62]; ExecuTorch exports models to a portable `.pte` artifact callable from C++, Swift or Java, hit 1.0 GA on 2025-10-22, and ships 12+ backends from Apple CoreML/MLX to ARM Ethos-U microcontrollers [63], with its paper describing a PyTorch-native framework scaling from MCUs to complex SoCs [64] and benchmarks on Qwen3 0.6B, Llama 3.2 1B and Phi4 Mini with group-wise 4-bit weights and dynamic 8-bit activations against ONNX Runtime, llama.cpp and LiteRT [65]. Qualcomm AI Hub offers 300+ pre-optimized validated models plus GenieX for running frontier LLMs locally on Hexagon NPU, Adreno GPU or CPU, used by over 1,800 companies [66][67]; ONNX Runtime provides a vendor-neutral path via CUDA/DirectML/CoreML/NNAPI execution providers but is described as heavier than purpose-built solutions, and MLC-LLM gives a TVM-based multi-platform alternative [68]. The corresponding survey advice is ExecuTorch for mobile production, llama.cpp for desktop/prototyping and MLX for the Apple ecosystem [55]. Academic systems work pushes the same frontier: Transformer-Lite for mobile phone GPUs [69], mllm-NPU targeting 1000 tokens/s on-device prefill via NPU offloading [8], LLMCad for model collaboration with speculative token generation [70], and characterization of mobile SoCs for heterogeneous GPU+NPU inference [71]. Device-level evaluations report that memory constraints — not processing power — pose the greater challenge for on-device LLMs [72][73].

Efficiency also matters on the datacenter side, where serving is one of the most power-intensive workloads [74]. A longitudinal study found vLLM software improvements (0.5.4 to 0.11.1) delivered 3–5x higher token throughput and 15–41% lower energy per output token on Llama 3.1 across H100s, e.g. the 8B model dropping from 0.20 J to 0.12 J per token at batch size 64, with the gap narrowing at larger batches as the GPU fills up; energy improves less than throughput because power draw rises as the GPU is kept busier [75]. BEAM co-optimizes batching and DVFS by jointly tuning GPU frequency, chunk size and microbatch count atop vLLM to save energy under SLOs [74]. A 1,858-configuration study found energy is governed by latent factors such as memory availability and hardware utilization, and that task type dominates: problem-solving tasks consumed 25x more energy per response than text conversation (Qwen 3 32B on a single B200: 11x more output tokens, 23x more energy) [76].

## Trends and open problems

Several tensions define the current research frontier. First, **scale-dependent compression sensitivity**: the same quantization that is nearly free on a 70B model can be costly on a 1B model, because outlier-handling methods transfer poorly to low-capacity models [11], and low-bit PTQ disproportionately raises method and execution errors in mathematical reasoning — failures emerge early and cascade, with drops up to 69.81% in harder settings — though as few as 332 curated examples and 3–5 minutes on one GPU can recover much of the loss while preserving PTQ efficiency [12]. Degradation also amplifies with task difficulty: for a 4-bit GPTQ reasoning model the accuracy loss was 0% on GSM8K, 1.40% on MATH-500 and 4.17% on AIME-120, roughly a fourfold amplification across tiers [13]. A 2026 triple-bottom-line comparison directly contrasts natively trained SLMs against post-training-quantized LLMs on edge AI across accuracy, latency, memory, energy and safety [77].

Second, **the reality of emergent ability at small scale**. The original emergent-abilities work reported that chain-of-thought gains helped only models above roughly 100B parameters, with multi-step arithmetic, MMLU and word-unscrambling near random below a threshold and rising sharply after it [78]. The "mirage" critique argues many such jumps are artifacts of harsh, discontinuous metrics like exact match, and that continuous metrics reveal smooth improvement [79][80]. SLM results such as phi-3-mini at 69% MMLU [9] and phi-1's 50.6% HumanEval at 1.3B [14] sit uneasily with a hard 100B threshold and are a live empirical question.

Third, **trustworthiness does not shrink with the model**. A study of 13 state-of-the-art SLMs finds most are highly susceptible to jailbreak attacks and some are vulnerable even to direct harmful prompts, and it analyzes security degradation caused by architecture compression, quantization and distillation [81] — a compounding risk given how central those techniques are to SLM deployment. Bias evaluations report small and medium LLMs exhibit bias across demographic categories, with Phi-3.5B least biased among those tested [82].

Fourth, **evaluation economics and agentic use**. tinyBenchmarks shows MMLU accuracy can be estimated from only 100 curated examples out of 14K, releasing tiny versions of MMLU, HELM, AlpacaEval 2.0 and the Open LLM Leaderboard [83] — useful when the model itself is cheap but evaluation is not. Agentic deployment is emerging as the strongest natural fit: a position paper argues SLMs are sufficiently powerful, inherently more suitable and more economical for the many specialized, repetitive invocations in agentic systems, and outlines an LLM-to-SLM agent conversion algorithm [84]; surveys of SLM agentic systems emphasize tool use, function calling and RAG under schema/API constraints with lower cost, latency and energy [85]; and evidence shows SLMs can be fine-tuned into effective search agents [86] and trained with multi-round RL and dual data flywheels for industrial-scale tool use [87]. Finally, the SLM survey literature itself identifies architecture, dataset and training-algorithm design — plus benchmarking latency and memory footprint rather than accuracy alone — as the open agenda [16][17][18], while the serving literature continues to push on routing, cascading and long-context memory hierarchies [19][20][38].
