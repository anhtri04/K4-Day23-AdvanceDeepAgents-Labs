# World Models: A Citation-Backed Survey of Architectures, Control, Generative Simulators and Open Problems

## TL;DR

- A world model is a learned predictive model of how an environment evolves under actions; the modern field starts from Ha & Schmidhuber's 2018 Vision–Memory–Controller decomposition [1][2] and now spans at least six distinct architectural families — latent-recurrent state-space models, value-equivalent planning models, autoregressive-token transformers, diffusion/generative-frame models, joint-embedding predictive architectures, and video-generation foundation models [3].
- Two lineages dominate control: latent imagination (PlaNet's RSSM → Dreamer/DreamerV2/DreamerV3, which train actor-critic only on imagined rollouts) [4][5][6][7] and planning with a learned model (MuZero's MCTS over a reward/policy/value model [8]; TD-MPC2's MPPI planning in a decoder-free latent space [9]). DreamerV3 reported Atari gamer median 830% versus MuZero's 693% with one configuration across 150+ tasks [10][7].
- Generative video models have become the most visible "world models" — Sora's spacetime-patch diffusion transformers [11], the Genie 1→2→3 progression to real-time 720p at 24 FPS [12][13][14], GameNGen playing DOOM at >20 FPS with PSNR 29.4 [15], and DIAMOND reaching a 1.46 human-normalized mean on Atari 100k [16] — but OpenAI itself concedes Sora "does not accurately model the physics of many basic interactions" [11].
- The sharpest open disagreement is definitional and methodological: Physics-IQ finds physical understanding "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" [17]; LeCun argues that modelling the world by generating pixels is "wasteful and doomed to failure" [18][19]; while Xing's "Critique of World Model" counters that latent prediction without a generative validator risks losing contact with reality and proposes Generative Latent Prediction [20][21].
- Evaluation is the field's weakest link: RL-style work scores task return [6][8][9] while generative work scores open-loop FID/FVD/PSNR [22], and both suffer from compounding error [23][24], model exploitation [23], hallucination [25], and non-standardized, sometimes non-reproducible benchmarks [26][27].

## Background

A world model, in the sense used across this literature, is a learned predictive representation of how an environment evolves, capable of supporting policy learning, planning, simulation, evaluation and data generation [28]. Several surveys formulate it as a triple of inference, dynamics and observation models — encoding observations into a state, rolling the state forward given actions, and decoding (or not decoding) back to observations [29] — and distinguish *explicit* world models that learn structured dynamics for rollout-based reasoning from *implicit* world models that encode predictive structure inside scalable learned representations [30]. The field matters now because general world models are being positioned as "a crucial pathway toward achieving AGI" [31], because foundation-scale interactive simulators are appearing at 720p/24 FPS in real time [14], and because a dense cluster of dedicated surveys has appeared between 2024 and 2026 to try to impose order on a rapidly fragmenting literature [32][33][3]. That fragmentation is itself a finding: one 3D/4D survey states plainly that the absence of a standardized definition and taxonomy for "world models" has led to fragmented and inconsistent claims [34], while a 2026 perspective article notes there is "no consensus on what a world model fundamentally is, what it should predict, or how it should be built" [23].

## Foundations: from the V-M-C decomposition to latent state-space and JEPA

The canonical starting point factorizes an agent into three separately trained parts: a Vision VAE (V) compressing each frame into a low-dimensional latent `z`, an MDN-RNN (M) modelling `P(z_{t+1} | a_t, z_t, h_t)` as a mixture of Gaussians, and a deliberately tiny linear Controller (C) mapping `[z_t, h_t]` to actions, so that most complexity resides in the world model and the controller can be trained entirely inside the model's "dream" [1][2]. Contemporary commentaries stress that this decomposition is older than the 2018 paper — Schmidhuber proposed RNN-based world models with separate controllers from 1990 onward, and Sutton's Dyna (1990) is the earlier model-based-RL antecedent — and that the 2018 contribution was showing the decomposition works on raw pixels with modern deep-learning components [35][21]. The reported numbers are concrete: `z ∈ R^32` for CarRacing and `z ∈ R^64` for VizDoom, a single linear layer with roughly 867 / 1,088 parameters trained by CMA-ES, and 906 ± 21 on CarRacing-v0 and 1092 ± 556 on VizDoom for agents trained purely in dream [35][21]. A widely cited cautionary detail is that an agent dreaming in VizDoom learned to avoid "fireballs" purely by exploiting a hole in the world model's physics, fixed only by raising the MDN-RNN sampling temperature τ [35][2].

The Dreamer lineage replaced explicit planning or controller search with a latent state-space backbone. PlaNet introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) with deterministic and stochastic transition components, planned online in latent space via the cross-entropy method, and proposed multi-step variational inference ("latent overshooting"), reaching approximately 50× the data efficiency of model-free D4PG on DeepMind Control Suite [4][21]. Dreamer then learned behaviours by "propagating analytic gradients of learned state values back through trajectories imagined in the compact state space of a learned world model," evaluated on 20 visual control tasks [5]; DreamerV2 switched to discrete categorical latents and became "the first agent that achieves human-level performance on the Atari benchmark of 55 tasks by learning behaviors inside a separately trained world model," surpassing the final performance of Rainbow and IQN with the same experience and computation [6][36]; DreamerV3 added normalization, balancing and transformation techniques that let a single configuration outperform specialists on over 150 tasks and become the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [7][10]. Mechanically, one imagined step is a GRU update plus a dynamics-predictor sample and reward/continue heads, with an RSSM state of a deterministic `h_t` (8192 floats) and stochastic `z_t` (32 categorical variables × 64 classes) — categorical because DreamerV2 found categorical latents beat Gaussian ones on Atari [10]. The backbone itself has been contested: S4WM shows structured state-space (S4) models delivering better long-term memory and efficiency than RNN- and Transformer-based alternatives in MBRL [37], and HRSSM continues to build on the RSSM while adding spatio-temporal masking, bisimulation and latent reconstruction to survive exogenous distractions [38].

The genuinely different lineage is the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture. I-JEPA is explicitly "a non-generative approach for self-supervised learning from images" that predicts the *representations* of target blocks from a single context block — prediction in representation space, with no reconstruction — and can train a ViT-Huge/14 on ImageNet in under 72 hours on 16 A100s [39]. V-JEPA extends the same feature-prediction objective to video without pretrained image encoders, text, negatives or reconstruction, trained on 2 million videos and reaching 81.9% on Kinetics-400, 72.2% on Something-Something-v2 and 77.9% on ImageNet1K with a ViT-H/16 [40]. V-JEPA 2 scales pre-training to over 1 million hours of internet video (77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2, 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100, 84.0 PerceptionTest and 76.9 TempCompass at 8B) and then post-trains a *latent action-conditioned world model*, V-JEPA 2-AC, on under 62 hours of unlabeled robot video from Droid, deployed zero-shot on Franka arms for pick-and-place with image goals and no task-specific reward [41]. The comparison to Dreamer is instructive: both predict forward in latent space, but Dreamer-style agents are usually trained with reconstruction plus reward and optimized for control, whereas JEPA is trained purely self-supervised with no external reward and optimized for prediction fidelity in representation space [42].

## Control and planning: latent imagination versus search in a learned model

The control literature splits into two paradigms. In the first, a policy is learned by imagination: DreamerV2's actor and critic are trained from imagined latent trajectories seeded at encoded states of previously encountered sequences [36], and DreamerV3 imagines 15 steps ahead from each replayed state, sampling 16,384 imagined states per update without ever touching pixels [10]. In the second, control is derived by *searching* a learned model at decision time. MuZero combines MCTS with a learned model that, applied iteratively, predicts "the quantities most directly relevant to planning: the reward, the action-selection policy, and the value function" — no pixel reconstruction — achieving a new state of the art on all 57 Atari games "in which model-based planning approaches have historically struggled," beating model-free R2D2 in 42 of 57 and matching AlphaZero at Go, chess and shogi without knowledge of the rules [8]. TD-MPC2 occupies a middle position: it performs local trajectory optimization in the latent space of a learned *implicit, decoder-free* world model using MPC with Model Predictive Path Integral sampling plus value bootstrapping beyond the short plan, and with a single hyperparameter set outperforms SAC and DreamerV3 across 104 online RL tasks spanning DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2 and MyoSuite; a single 317M-parameter agent performs 80 tasks across multiple embodiments and action spaces up to `A ∈ R^39` [9].

Scaled comparisons are informative but should be read as one-off reports rather than controlled studies: DreamerV3's reported Atari gamer median of 830% against MuZero's 693%, 66.01 versus 64.89 on ProcGen against PPG, 125% versus IRIS's 105% on Atari100k, and 66% versus Boot DQN's 60% on BSuite [10]. On the robotics side, the coupling between world model and policy is now the organizing axis: IDM-style (video generator plus a separate inverse-dynamics model), single-backbone, and MoE/MoT designs where video and action experts interact through joint attention, plus latent-space prediction [28]. Reported LIBERO manipulation success rates in one compilation reach a four-suite average of 98.1 (Say-Dream-ACT: Spatial 99.4, Object 99.2, Goal 98.6, Long 95.4) — though the survey explicitly flags this as a compilation and not a controlled causal comparison, and notes long-horizon manipulation remains the differentiator [28]. Recent work pushes on the planner side rather than the model side: SAGE uses goal-conditioned latent subgoals to guide action-sequence generation inside a *frozen* world model, improving long-horizon planning [43]. World models are also distinguished by *role*: as a "sandbox" for policy improvement, where imagined transitions update a policy, versus a "judge" for evaluation or decision-time selection, where rollouts score candidate behaviour without retraining [28].

## Generative video world models and neural simulators

The most visible branch of the field reframes video generation as simulation. OpenAI trains text-conditional diffusion models jointly on videos and images of variable duration, resolution and aspect ratio using a transformer over "spacetime patches" of latent codes, with the largest model, Sora, generating up to a minute of high-fidelity video; the report claims emergent 3D consistency, long-range coherence, object permanence, world interaction (a painter leaving persistent strokes; a man eating a burger leaving bite marks) and zero-shot simulation of digital worlds such as Minecraft — while explicitly stating that "Sora currently exhibits numerous limitations as a simulator" and "does not accurately model the physics of many basic inter[actions]" [11]. Critical reading of that claim matters: Millière characterizes OpenAI's announcement as "marketing documents rather than academic articles," places Sora as a Diffusion Transformer replacing the U-Net with a modified ViT over latent patch tokens, and concludes it "does not run simulations in the traditional sense," though it may represent limited physical properties [44].

The Genie line is the clearest progression toward interactivity. Genie is an 11B-parameter unsupervised model producing action-controllable virtual worlds from unlabelled videos via spatiotemporal tokenization and autoregressive dynamics [12]; Genie 2 generates action-controllable, playable 3D environments from a single prompt image, consistent "for up to a minute" (most examples 10–20 s), with emergent action controls, counterfactual trajectories, long-horizon memory, object affordances, NPC modelling and physics such as water, smoke and gravity [13]; Genie 3 generates dynamic worlds from a text prompt navigable in real time at 24 frames per second, retaining consistency for a few minutes at 720p resolution, and is presented as a stepping stone to AGI via unlimited curricula of simulated environments [14]. Diffusion-frame engines provide the strongest existence proof for interactivity: GameNGen interactively simulates DOOM at over 20 FPS on a single TPU, its next-frame prediction achieves PSNR 29.4 (comparable to lossy JPEG), and human raters are only slightly better than chance at distinguishing short clips of the real game from the simulation, using an augmented Stable Diffusion v1.4 with ~4 denoising steps while maintaining game state over long periods despite ~3 seconds of effective context [15].

The architectural comparison that runs through this branch is autoregressive tokens versus diffusion frames. DIAMOND argues that the prevailing design — sequences of discrete latent variables — may discard visual details that matter for RL, and shows a diffusion world model capturing that detail while reaching a mean human-normalized score of 1.46 on Atari 100k, a new state of the art for agents trained entirely within a world model, at 13M parameters (versus IRIS's 30M and DreamerV3's 18M), though training took 2.9 days versus DreamerV3's under a day [16]. In autonomous driving the split is by representation rather than by generator: GAIA-1 is described as the first generative world model designed specifically for driving, using video, text and action inputs and unsupervised sequence modelling, with a 9-billion-parameter model trained on 4,700 hours of UK driving data and a 6.5B-parameter world-model component trained for 15 days on 64 A100s, converting future prediction into next-token prediction [45]. OccWorld instead learns a world model in 3D occupancy space, using a reconstruction-based scene tokenizer to obtain discrete scene tokens and a GPT-like spatio-temporal generative transformer to produce subsequent scene and ego tokens decoded into future occupancy and ego trajectory, giving competitive planning on nuScenes without instance and map supervision [46]. For embodied agents, UniSim learns a universal action-conditioned real-world simulator from heterogeneous video enabling zero-shot transfer to vision-language planning, RL and video captioning [47], and Genie Envisioner pairs a video diffusion model with a neural simulator for instruction-driven manipulation instead of a conventional physics engine [48]. The paradigm has even attracted a maturity roadmap: one position paper proposes Interactive Generative Video as the basis for Generative Game Engines, with a hierarchical L0–L4 roadmap [49].

## Taxonomy: what the surveys disagree about

The survey cluster itself is a useful object of study, because the disagreements are in the organizing axes rather than the applications. The broadest general surveys split the field by function — "understanding world or predicting future?", i.e. constructing internal representations to understand mechanisms versus predicting future states to simulate and guide decisions — and enumerate generative games, autonomous driving, robotics and social simulacra as domains [32]. A 2026 survey instead proposes four axes, separating architecture (representation format, dynamics formulation, input modality, learning paradigm, downstream application) from methodological family (state-space/recurrent, transformer, diffusion generators, physics-informed networks, language-augmented multimodal systems), reasoning strategy (imagination-based planning, latent policy learning, counterfactual reasoning, planning under uncertainty) and application domain [33]. An embodied-AI survey uses three axes — functionality (decision-coupled vs general-purpose), temporal modelling (sequential simulation and inference vs global difference prediction) and spatial representation (global latent vector, token feature sequence, spatial latent grid, decomposed rendering) — and systematizes metrics across pixel prediction quality, state-level understanding and task performance [29]. A tutorial-style treatment reduces the space to explicit versus implicit world models [30]. Domain-scoped surveys diverge further: driving surveys propose either a three-tier process taxonomy (future physical world generation; behaviour planning; interaction between prediction and planning) [50] or a modality-based categorization (video, point cloud, occupancy, latent feature, traffic map) along with a review of simulators, datasets and metrics [51]. Representation-first surveys argue the field has over-emphasized 2D image/video generation and propose a VideoGen / OccGen / LiDARGen split over native 3D and 4D representations [34]; robot-centric surveys organize around world-model/policy coupling and simulator role [28]. A prominent 2026 blog-indexed survey organizes more than 160 systems across six modelling approaches and six application domains, and usefully distinguishes a *world model* (predicts what happens next, may propose an action) from a *world-action model* (also produces the actions to reach a desired future) [3]. One roadmap paper instead conceptualizes video foundation models as an implicit world model plus a video renderer and traces four generations deepening three abilities — faithfulness, interactiveness and planning [52]. Finally, the 2024 Sora-era survey is visibly narrower, scoping around video generation, autonomous-driving world models and autonomous agents [31], whereas later surveys add scientific modelling, medical imaging, educational measurement and finance [33][3].

Two conclusions follow from comparing them rather than reading any one alone. First, the taxonomies are largely orthogonal, not competing: a system is simultaneously characterizable by methodological family [3][33], by function (understanding vs predicting) [32], by spatial representation [29][34], by coupling to a policy [28] and by simulator role [28]. Second, the *convergence* across surveys is stronger than the divergence — every one of them lists compounding prediction error and long-horizon drift, physical/causal consistency versus pixel fidelity, sim-to-real transfer, fragmented evaluation, and compute cost for real-time interaction as the central obstacles [3][32][29][33][28]. The definitional dispute, by contrast, is genuinely unresolved: OpenWorldLib explicitly excludes text-to-video models such as Sora, arguing that video generation shows some grasp of physical relationships but "is missing the crucial feedback loop with the real world," and defines a world model as grounded in perception, able to interact and capable of long-term memory, with core task areas of interactive video generation, multimodal reasoning and vision-language-action [53]. That places it on LeCun's side of the argument [18][19].

## Trends and open problems

**Physical understanding does not follow from visual realism.** Physics-IQ, a benchmark that can only be solved by understanding fluid dynamics, optics, solid mechanics, magnetism and thermodynamics, evaluates Sora, Runway, Pika, Lumiere, Stable Video Diffusion and VideoPoet and finds physical understanding "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism," concluding that "visual realism does not imply physical understanding" [17]. The same benchmark is now being audited: Physics-IQ Verified refines 57.6% of all samples and improves over 34.8% of prompts, and across six image-to-video models it observes "moderate but meaningful ranking changes" (Kendall's τ = 0.46) — a warning that benchmark construction, not just model quality, determines rankings [54]. Separately reported figures put no model above 60 on a scale where 100 means parity with reality [55]. Intuitive-physics evaluation traces a similar arc: IntPhys uses the violation-of-expectation paradigm and finds best-model error rates as low as 0.07 on visible object-permanence tasks but performance plummeting "when the physical violations occurred behind an occluder" [56], and IntPhys 2 extends this to object permanence, immutability, spatio-temporal continuity and solidity with matched possible/impossible pairs so that surface cues cannot pass [57].

**Compounding error, model exploitation and hallucination are now measurable.** Autoregressive rollouts feed the model its own predictions, so one-step inaccuracies accumulate, the imagined state distribution drifts off the training manifold, and error "grows superlinearly in horizon"; planners systematically exploit the model's optimistic errors — "model exploitation" [23]. WoVR identifies two compounding causes — autoregressive feedback and distribution shift as the evolving policy's action distribution drifts from the world model's training data — and proposes stabilized autoregressive context modelling, keyframe-initialized rollouts to shorten effective prediction depth, and world-model/policy co-evolution; it also notes agents can "cheat" by exploiting an unaligned simulator's hallucinations, and that inverse-dynamics action inference is ill-posed because multiple actions explain the same transition [24]. Hallucination itself has been taxonomized with a 350M-parameter generative world model (video tokenizer plus action-conditioned dynamics plus decoder) trained on MMBench2, a 427-hour corpus of 210 tasks across 10 domains: three modes — perceptual (the tokenizer snaps unseen observations to the nearest known scene), action marginalization (the dynamics ignores the action and regenerates the same trajectory) and scene divergence (abrupt drift in low-coverage state space, e.g. teleporting or penetrating walls) — with the argument that hallucination is "first and foremost, a data-coverage problem," and three label-free runtime predictors (tokenizer round-trip residual, flow instability, inter-seed variance) tracking realized rollout error at Spearman ρ ≈ 0.8 across 9k held-out sequences without labels or extra training [25].

**The definitional fight is a methodological fight.** LeCun's position is that modelling the world for action "by generating pixels is as wasteful and doomed to failure as the largely-abandoned idea of 'analysis by synthesis'" [18], and that pixel prediction is fundamentally under-determined because the information needed for the next frame is not present in the current frame, so probability distributions over high-dimensional pixel spaces collapse to blurry averages — JEPA instead predicts in an abstract space where an encoder discards unpredictable detail and retains plannable structure [19]. Xing's counter-position is that the primary goal of a world model is "simulating all actionable possibilities of the real world for purposeful reasoning and acting," and that abstract latent prediction without a generative validator is "essentially meditation in a closed room," prone to losing contact with reality; the proposed remedy is a Generative Latent Prediction architecture with stateful, hierarchical, multi-level and mixed continuous/discrete representations inside a Physical, Agentic and Nested (PAN) system [20][21]. Notably both camps agree on the shared skeleton — encode observations into a state, roll the state forward given actions, extract decisions or rewards [21] — which suggests the dispute is about where the supervisory signal and the decoder belong, not about the existence of latent dynamics.

**Evaluation and reproducibility remain the field's structural weak point.** A decision-making-centric position paper argues the term "world model" now covers action-conditioned environment models, latent imagination models, future-video predictors, interactive neural simulators, latent predictive representations and synthetic-data engines, and identifies a recurring "claim/evidence mismatch" in which papers make stronger usefulness claims than their evaluation can establish; it proposes an L0–L7 ladder from visual plausibility to policy-optimization utility, foregrounding interventional action fidelity, closed-loop rollout validity, reward/value prediction, policy-ranking agreement, optimization lift, model exploitability and uncertainty calibration, and contrasts video-generation metrics (FID, FVD, aesthetics, image quality, human preference) with open-loop world-model metrics (MSE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DreamSim, latent L2, temporal/optical-flow) [22]. The MBRL benchmarking literature made this complaint years earlier: research "has not been very standardized," authors commonly experiment on self-designed environments, several lines of work are closed-source or not reproducible, and the field's challenges include the dynamics bottleneck, the planning-horizon dilemma and the early-termination dilemma — with no clear consistently best model-based RL algorithm even given substantial benchmarking [26]. The response is now infrastructure: WorldArena 2.0 extends embodied benchmarking beyond vision-only simulators to multimodal perception, interactive RL environments and diverse robotic platforms [58], and stable-worldmodel unifies data loading, world-model implementations and standardized evaluation specifically to address the reproducibility gap [27].

**Where the field is heading.** Four directions recur across the notes. (i) Native 3D/4D representations — video streams, occupancy grids and LiDAR point clouds under geometric, action-based and semantic conditions — as an alternative to 2D-centric generation [34][46]. (ii) Action-conditioned interactive generation, including streaming panoramic world models that interpret each instruction as camera motion and stream a minute of 2K 360-degree video in real time [59][53], and physics-informed or constrained learning with counterfactual reasoning and long-horizon hierarchical planning [23]. (iii) Unified multimodal and foundation-scale interactive simulators, with safe deployment in safety-critical domains [33][23], and with the world-action model framing absorbing action generation into the model itself [3]. (iv) Hardware and architecture reconsideration: the OpenWorldLib authors argue that today's token-centric chips are mismatched to world-model needs and that new architectures, possibly moving beyond the Transformer, may be required [53] — a claim whose practical counterpart is that interactive models such as Genie 3 and GameNGen converge on roughly 20–24 FPS as the usability bar while their effective context remains only seconds [15][14]. Interpretability is a smaller but active front: transformers trained on maze-solving tasks develop structured causal world models analyzable with sparse autoencoders and attention patterns, with the learned models influenced by positional encoding schemes [60] — although SAE-based interpretability has itself been challenged by work arguing that sparse autoencoders "fail to reliably decompose neural network internals despite strong reconstruction performance" [61].
